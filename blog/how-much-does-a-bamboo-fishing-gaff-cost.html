A bamboo fishing gaff costs anywhere from about $50 to $300. Custom painted or showpiece builds can run $500 or more. That's a wide range for what looks like a stick with a hook on it. The price comes down to four things: the length of the Calcutta cane, the hook, how the gaff is wrapped and finished, and whether it was built by hand or on a production line.

This guide breaks down what you actually pay for, what a fair price looks like at each length, where cheap gaffs cut corners, and how to get the most gaff for your money.

What goes into the price of a bamboo gaff

1. The Calcutta cane

Real Calcutta bamboo (sometimes called "iron cane") is what makes a bamboo gaff worth owning. It's light, it floats, and it flexes like a fishing rod, so it absorbs the thrash of a hot fish instead of fighting it. Not every cane is gaff-grade, though. Builders sort through bundles for straight, thick-walled, crack-free sections with tight nodes. Longer, heavier-walled blanks are rarer, which is a big reason a 6' gaff costs noticeably more than a 3'.

2. The hook

The hook is the part that actually touches the fish, and it's where cheap gaffs cut the most corners. Common options, from least to most expensive:

  • Mustad gaff hooks (2/0–5/0) — proven, sharp and easy to maintain. A ceramic coating adds corrosion resistance and color.
  • 316 stainless steel — marine-grade and holds up very well in saltwater. A good choice if you rinse your gear irregularly.
  • Titanium — the lightest and most corrosion-proof option, and the most expensive.

Hook gap matters too. A 2.5" gap suits smaller fish close to the boat, while 3"–4" gaps are for tuna and bigger pelagics. A larger gap usually adds a few dollars.

3. Length and action

Every extra foot of cane costs more, and so does a stiffer (heavier-walled) blank. A 3' gaff is great for kayaks, skiffs and fish brought right to the rail. A 5'–6' gaff reaches past a high gunwale or a swim step on a sportfisher. If you're not sure which length you need, see our guide on how to choose gaff size.

4. Wrap, grip and finish

A good wrap does more than look good. Cord or paracord wrapped tightly over the cane gives you grip when your hands are wet and slimy, and it protects the bamboo. The finish (sealing and epoxy over the wrap) keeps water out of the cane so it doesn't crack or rot. That's hours of hand work per gaff, and it's the part you can't see in a product photo.

5. Handmade vs. mass-produced

A handmade gaff is built one at a time, so the builder chooses the cane, sets the hook, wraps it and checks it before it ships. That labor is the difference between a $60 gaff and a $180 gaff. For a tool you're trusting with a fish of a lifetime, it's usually worth paying for.

Why are bamboo gaffs so expensive compared to aluminum?

An aluminum or fiberglass gaff can be cut, fitted and packaged by a machine in minutes. A Calcutta gaff can't. The cane has to be sorted, cut, fitted, sealed, wrapped and finished by hand. What you get for that money:

  • It floats. Drop a bamboo gaff overboard and you get it back. Drop an aluminum one and it's gone.
  • It flexes. The cane absorbs head shakes and surges, so it's less likely to tear out of a fish or wrench your wrist.
  • It's light. It's easy to swing fast and accurately at the end of a long fight.
  • It's easy on your boat. Bamboo won't gouge gelcoat the way a metal shaft can.
  • It's one of a kind. No two canes are identical, and a handmade wrap turns a tool into something you want to hang on the wall.

Is a bamboo gaff worth it?

If you fish saltwater more than a few times a year, usually yes. A cheap gaff that bends, loses its hook or rusts out costs you twice: once when you buy it, and again when it fails with a good fish at the boat. A well-built Calcutta gaff that's rinsed and stored properly will last for years of trips.

A budget gaff can make sense if you're only fishing a couple of times a season, or if you want a backup that lives on the boat and gets abused. Just check the hook and how it's attached before you trust it with a big fish.

How to save money on a bamboo gaff (without buying junk)

  • Buy the right length the first time. Measure from your gunwale to the waterline. Most people who end up buying two gaffs bought the wrong length first.
  • Match the hook to your fishing. You don't need titanium for bay bass or kayak halibut. A ceramic-coated Mustad hook is a great value for most inshore and nearshore fishing.
  • Take care of it. Rinse it with fresh water after every trip, let it dry, and store it out of direct sun. Our bamboo gaff care guide covers the details. A gaff you look after will last years longer than one you don't.
  • Build a matching set over time. Start with the gaff, then add a matching tuna spike or deck brush later.

DIY bamboo gaff vs. buying one

You can build your own. Calcutta blanks, a gaff hook, epoxy and cord will run roughly $40–$100 in materials, plus tools and a weekend of work. It's a fun project if you like building gear, and our build your own fishing gaff post walks through it.

The catch is that the hard parts — picking a sound blank, seating the hook so it can't pull out, and sealing the cane so it doesn't crack — are exactly what you're paying a builder for. If the gaff is going to meet a big tuna, most anglers would rather it be built by someone who has done it hundreds of times.

Bamboo fishing gaff cost: FAQ

How much does a bamboo fishing gaff cost?

Most quality handmade bamboo gaffs cost $150–$300 depending on length and hook. Budget imports start around $30. Fully custom art gaffs can cost $500 or more.

Why is a 6' gaff so much more expensive than a 3' gaff?

Long, straight, heavy-walled Calcutta blanks are much harder to find than short ones. A longer gaff also needs more wrap, more finishing and more careful shipping.

Is a stainless or titanium hook worth the upgrade?

Stainless (316) is a great upgrade if your gear doesn't always get rinsed. Titanium is the lightest and most corrosion-proof option, and it's worth it if you want a gaff to last for decades with minimal care.

Do bamboo gaffs float?

Yes. Calcutta bamboo is naturally buoyant, which is one of the biggest reasons offshore anglers choose it over aluminum.

How long does a bamboo gaff last?

A well-sealed Calcutta gaff that's rinsed after use and stored out of the sun can last many seasons. Check the wrap and the hook seat regularly, and touch up the hook point with a file.
